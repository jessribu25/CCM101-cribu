# Reflection

### 1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?

Google Cloud Platform impressed me the most. I was surprised to learn that Kubernetes, which I already knew was widely used in the industry, originally began as an internal project at Google before becoming open-source. This gave me a better appreciation of GCP and showed me that its strengths come from Google's own experience in developing large-scale technologies.

### 2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?

Although AWS, Azure, and GCP have different names and features, they provide many of the same basic cloud services. All three offer computing, storage, networking, databases, and identity management. They also use pay-as-you-go pricing, provide free-tier options, and operate their infrastructure across different regions and availability zones around the world.

### 3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?

I learned that choosing a cloud provider depends on the specific needs of an organization rather than simply choosing the platform considered the best overall. For example, a university that already uses Microsoft 365 may find Azure more suitable because of its strong Microsoft integration. Meanwhile, an organization focused on AI research may prefer GCP because of its machine learning capabilities. This shows that there is no single cloud provider that is the best choice for every organization.

### 4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?

Creating recommendations for different fictional clients taught me to think beyond simply identifying cloud services. I had to explain why a particular service would be appropriate for a specific business requirement. This helped me understand how cloud professionals connect technical solutions with actual business needs.

### 5. How has your GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio improved after completing this mission?

After completing three labs, I can see clear progress in my portfolio. Lab 1 focused on basic Linux commands, Lab 2 involved exploring and understanding infrastructure, and Lab 3 focused on comparing cloud platforms and making recommendations. Instead of looking like separate activities, the labs now show my gradual development of cloud computing knowledge and decision-making skills.
